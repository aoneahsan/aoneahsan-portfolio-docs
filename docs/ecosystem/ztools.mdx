ZTools gathers developer and creator utilities in one searchable browser toolbox. Its published set includes formatters, converters, encoders and generators.

**The question:** Where should a one-off developer utility live?

## What is the entry point?

A person can search the toolbox for the utility they need. That is more useful than scattering small tools across unrelated product sites.

## Where does the work run?

The roster says the tools run in the browser and work offline. I would inspect a specific utility before making a stronger data-flow claim about it.

## What stays consistent?

Search and dark mode bring the tool catalogue together.

## Why put these small tools together?

A searchable toolbox gives a person one place to find a formatter, converter, encoder or generator instead of following unrelated links for each job. The roster says the work runs in the browser, works offline and has a dark-mode-first interface. I would open one utility, inspect its input and output, and then check the same flow without a connection before repeating the offline claim more broadly. The product link points to the current catalogue, which is more useful than promising a fixed count of utilities. A tool's job is to make one transformation clear and predictable, then get out of the way.

The specific tool page should name its input limits and show the result before a person trusts it with another file.

## From the release record

- A toolbox of developer and creator utilities, one click away.
- Formatters, converters, encoders and generators in one place
- Everything runs in your browser — nothing is uploaded
- Searchable, dark-mode-first, works offline
