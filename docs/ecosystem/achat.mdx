AChat makes the first contact a choice. I can point to its web and Android app, and to the three ways someone can find another person without exchanging a phone number.

**The question:** How can people connect without sharing a phone number?

## What changes at the first invitation?

A username, invitation link or QR code gives a person a way to reach someone they chose. That is a narrower request than handing over a phone number. The product also has anonymous rooms that can be opened without an account.

## What can a visitor inspect?

The released web app is the direct destination here. The roster also records an Android surface. I would check those actual entry points before describing a messaging experience as shipped.

## Where is the boundary?

The published facts describe discovery and anonymous rooms. They do not promise that every conversation is anonymous or that no information is ever stored. A useful portfolio page should keep that distinction visible.

## Why keep these ways of meeting separate?

A username, an invite link and a QR code all solve the first contact, but they ask different things of the people using them. The published release gives those choices alongside an anonymous room that does not need an account. I would inspect each entry point on the live web app, then check the Android surface recorded for the same product. The useful engineering question is whether a person can tell which kind of conversation they are entering before they share an address or start a room. The roster gives the routes into a chat. It does not settle every privacy question about the chat afterward.

## From the release record

- AChat is a messaging app on the web and Android for chosen people.
- People can find a user by username, invite link or QR code without a phone number.
- Anonymous rooms can be opened without an account.
