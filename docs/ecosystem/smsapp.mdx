SMS App uses a person’s own Android device to send messages. The published flows include single, batch and scheduled sends, with device registration and volunteer-pool management.

**The question:** Where does a scheduled SMS actually leave from?

## What is the sending device?

The Android phone is the sending endpoint. That is a concrete architecture choice, and it changes what a user should check before relying on a scheduled send.

## Which workflows are public?

Single messages, batches and schedules are in the roster. Device registration and volunteer-pool management are also recorded, but this page does not invent delivery guarantees.

## Where can the route be inspected?

The live product is linked here. I would verify a send against its actual Android receipt, not treat a queued status as proof that a phone sent the message.

## Where does a send become evidence?

At the registered Android device, not at the moment a web page says a job was queued. The published product has single, batch and scheduled messages, plus device registration and volunteer-pool management. Those are meaningful workflows, but they do not by themselves establish delivery to a recipient. I would inspect the device status, a scheduled item and the resulting send record before describing a message as sent. The architecture makes the phone an active endpoint. That gives a user control over the sending device and gives the product a responsibility to distinguish a planned send from an actual one.
